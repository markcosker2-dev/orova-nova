# OROVA Reel review 02 — The lead handoff

Status: owner-review draft only. Do not queue or publish.

Audience: West Coast custom builders and remodelers running or considering Meta lead ads.
Purpose: earn a save/DM from a builder who recognizes a broken handoff, not broad vanity reach.

## Voiceover

Custom builders: you paid for the click. Who followed up?

When a homeowner opts in through a Meta ad, the real work starts. Capture the project and location. Send a useful first reply. Offer a clear consultation. Record what happened.

If the trail disappears after the form, more ad spend won't fix the handoff. Check your last ten opted-in leads. Which step broke?

Save this checklist, or DM MAP and we'll map the path with you.

## Visual direction

- 9:16, voice-led, faceless, original motion design, about 25 seconds.
- A lead card travels through a compact four-stage path: form, fit, reply, consultation. Show one stalled path and then the repaired path.
- First two seconds: large kinetic type, no logo intro. Alternate near-black, off-white, and a single restrained OROVA green accent; use the supplied wordmark exactly as an ending signature.
- Use the voiceover as the master clock. Match one idea per visual beat and timed captions; avoid a silent or blank tail.
- Leave Instagram interface safe zones clear of essential words. Use dynamic graphic layouts, not the prior repeated full-screen card style.
- No invented customer story, results, numbers, or claim that OROVA already handled a lead.

## Instagram caption draft (not approved to publish)

Your Meta ad can do its job and still leave a builder with an empty calendar.

Trace the path after the form: project + location, useful first reply, consultation offer, and an outcome you can actually find later. If one step vanishes, fix that handoff before buying more clicks.
