## 2. THE headline: the ICP layer was decorative, and now isn't

Every licence-registry lead scored a flat **61**. Not because the weights were
wrong — because `find_leads_v3` emitted registry leads through a hand-built
whitelist (`licence_out`) that dropped exactly the three fields the hunt had
just finished RANKING on: `principal_count`, `insurance_amt`, `vertical`.

Everything upstream had always worked:

```
[WA_LNI] principals resolved for 25/25 businesses · 6 are sole operators
[WA_LNI] insurance resolved for 25/25 businesses · 1 carry above the $1M minimum
[WA_LNI] ranked 25 candidates — returning 5, 5 sole operators
```

Five sole operators, selected on cover, handed to a projection that discarded
both signals it had just sorted by. Fixed in #168. LEWCO went 76 → **83**.

**One field needed naming in SIX separate places** before it worked end to end.
That pattern is written up on its own — read
[[2026-08-16-a-whitelist-is-a-silent-contract]] before adding any scoring input.

## 6. Lessons worth carrying

> **A whitelist is a silent contract.** Six hand-built field lists had to name
> one field. Every omission read as "the registry has no data" and none raised.
> Full write-up: [[2026-08-16-a-whitelist-is-a-silent-contract]].

> **A flat, identical score means the scorer is receiving nothing.** 61 this
> time, 50 before ADR-0012. Both times the weights got blamed first.

> **Success and silence can look identical.** `hunt-leads` returned ok and did
> nothing; a GC'd task surfaces as CANCELLED and the callback skipped those.
> When something reports success, check it did work.

> **Instrument, don't theorise — and beware the instrument.** I twice concluded
> the registry joins were "failing silently" when they log correctly and the
> 100-line buffer had rotated past them. I also read a missing API key as a null
> value and reported stored data as absent. Polling *during* a run settled in
> seconds what inference got wrong twice.
