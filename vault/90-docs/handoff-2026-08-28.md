## 3. The strategy changed — ADR-0017

**Lead with the sample, not a claim.** There are no testimonials and none may
be invented, so the artefact persuades:

> *"We've already built the AI lead qualifier. Can I have it call you?"*

The prospect is qualified BY it, and books from inside the demo.

**Asking permission is also the legal cure.** A yes on a live call is prior
express consent under §227(b) — and CA PUC §2874 *requires* a live operator to
obtain consent before an automated system plays. The method landed on that
structure from sales instinct, not statute-reading.

**The flow Mark settled on (2026-08-27):** DM or email says a demo was built for
them. If interested they either **ring the number** (inbound, no ADAD question
at all) or **give a time** (which is itself the consent record). Inbound is the
better default: it works on all 82 leads today where outbound works on 5, it
filters for intent, and it runs while Mark sleeps across a 15-hour gap.

## 8. Lessons this session earned

> **Fail-open turns a dead dependency into an empty field, not an error.**
> Four failures, four empty fields, zero log lines.

> **A redaction that leaves the derivation is not a redaction.** The handoff
> that withheld the key explained how to rebuild it from the previous one.

> **Put the gate where the paths converge.** Self-review caught me putting the
> jurisdiction gate in the CLI — one function from the comment warning against
> exactly that.

> **A flat discount is not a flat percentage.** Copying −$6,000 from a $5k
> package onto a $3k one gave away two free months.
